# Accessible Workspaces: Design and Operations Checklist

**Purpose.** Make work possible as one accessible journey: understanding the site, reaching the work area, doing the work and using shared facilities, and getting support or leaving safely. Use this checklist for offices, production workrooms and other shared workspaces. Adapt task-specific equipment and safety arrangements to the actual work.

**Key.** P = physical access; N = non-visual, hearing or other sensory access; C = cognitive and neuroinclusive access. These broad labels help with scanning; they are not exhaustive, mutually exclusive or a way to infer an individual's needs.

**How to record a review.** For each stable item ID, record **Not assessed / In progress / Works as intended / Barrier found / Not applicable**. Explain any “Not applicable” decision. Record the workspace or project, location and task, evidence and its limits, action owner role, due date, resolution and verification. Keep personal or adjustment information in an approved restricted system, not in a public checklist or repository. The companion [finding record schema](workspace-finding.schema.json) defines a machine-readable format.

**Status of guidance.** Every item below is a **project review prompt and good-practice recommendation**. A prompt may help identify a legal, contractual or organisational duty, but it is not itself a statement of law or proof of compliance. In England, [Approved Document M, Volume 2](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-and-use-of-buildings-approved-document-m) guides compliance with Building Regulations for relevant building work. The [Equality Act reasonable-adjustments duty](https://www.gov.uk/reasonable-adjustments-for-disabled-workers) and [workplace health and safety duties](https://www.hse.gov.uk/disability/employers-duties/index.htm) also matter in operation. [BS 8300-1/-2](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/search?searchTerm=BS%208300) and [PAS 6463](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-GB/insights-and-media/insights/brochures/pas-6463-design-for-the-mind-neurodiversity-and-the-built-environment/) are design guidance, not a substitute for project-specific legal advice. Check jurisdiction, building use, alterations and fire strategy with competent specialists. Applicable obligations depend on the project and circumstances.

## 1. Knowing where you are

- [ ] **AW-01 (P/N/C)** Send an accessible pre-arrival guide: entrance, step-free route, parking/drop-off, public transport, reception, lifts, work areas, meeting rooms, toilets, break and quiet spaces, assistance-dog facilities and how to request an adjustment. State known temporary barriers and an alternative route.
- [ ] **AW-02 (N/C)** Provide accessible digital and on-site maps with clear room names, “you are here”, landmarks and routes. Use plain-language, large, legible signs in predictable locations; pair visual information with tactile information where useful. Do not rely on colour alone.
- [ ] **AW-03 (C/N)** Explain the sensory environment before arrival: likely noise, lighting, smells, crowded periods, and available quieter routes and spaces. Make reception and visitor procedures predictable, with a named contact and an alternative to a phone-only check-in.
- [ ] **AW-04 (N/C)** Keep room names, door numbers, booking systems and signs consistent. Update maps and instructions when furniture, routes or room uses change. QR codes, apps and indoor navigation can supplement physical information but must not be the only way to find a destination.

## 2. The route to where you work

- [ ] **AW-05 (P)** Provide a continuous, usable step-free route from arrival to every necessary work and welfare facility, including meeting rooms, kitchens, toilets and rest areas. Check clear door openings, manoeuvring space, thresholds, door forces and controls against applicable design guidance and actual users and equipment; a nominal door width alone does not establish access.
- [ ] **AW-06 (P/N)** Position door and lift controls consistently, visibly and within seated reach; make essential controls usable without fine dexterity. Provide visual and audible lift information and tactile controls where appropriate. Keep routes clear of boxes, bins, cables and temporary sets.
- [ ] **AW-07 (N/C)** Make changes in level, glazed doors, stairs and decision points identifiable through lighting, contrast, signs and appropriate tactile cues. Avoid glare and confusing surface patterns. Have a specialist design any tactile guiding surface so it does not create a trip or wheelchair barrier.
- [ ] **AW-08 (P/C)** Offer a quieter route where feasible. Ensure security gates, turnstiles and reception procedures have an equally dignified accessible route, usable during normal hours and out of hours.
- [ ] **AW-09 (P/N/C)** Inspect routes after layout changes and during busy periods. Include deliveries, cleaning, filming or event setups in the inspection; a route that works only when the building is empty is not reliable.

## 3. The work area and shared facilities

- [ ] **AW-10 (P)** Provide a choice of work positions with clear approach and turning space, reachable storage, sockets and controls, and tables suitable for different bodies and mobility equipment. Make height-adjustable desks, supportive seating, footrests or alternative input devices available where needed; avoid assuming one standard desk suits everyone.
- [ ] **AW-11 (P/N/C)** Assess each regular display-screen workstation with the worker, including home or hybrid setups where applicable. Check chair, screen, keyboard, input devices, lighting and task demands; revisit the assessment when the person, role or equipment changes. See [HSE's DSE assessment guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/dse/assessment.htm).
- [ ] **AW-12 (N/C)** Offer controllable task lighting, blinds and low-glare screens; manage flicker, reflections and abrupt light changes. Provide acoustic treatment and a choice of quieter focused-work spaces away from printers, kitchens and heavy footfall. Allow control over noise and visual distraction without isolating someone from their team.
- [ ] **AW-13 (N/C)** Ensure work software, booking systems, entry systems and shared devices work with the worker's assistive technology. Offer screen readers, magnification, speech input, captions, hearing support, alternative keyboards or other tools according to individual need. Permit personal settings to persist in shared or booked spaces where practicable.
- [ ] **AW-14 (P/N/C)** Make meeting rooms usable: accessible route and seating choice, wheelchair space within the group, controllable lighting, audible and visible participation, working hearing support where provided, and hybrid technology that supports captions and remote participation. Share agendas and materials in accessible formats ahead of time; describe meaningful visuals and give speakers time to contribute.
- [ ] **AW-15 (P/N/C)** Check kitchens, drinking water, rest spaces, lockers and toilets as part of the work journey. Put essential items within usable reach; provide easy-grip controls, legible labels and enough manoeuvring room. Keep accessible toilets available, clean and free of storage. Check transfer arrangements and any emergency assistance equipment against applicable guidance and users' needs. [HSE guidance on welfare facilities](https://www.hse.gov.uk/simple-health-safety/workplace-facilities/health-safety.htm) includes provision suitable for disabled workers.
- [ ] **AW-16 (C)** Offer a genuinely low-stimulation break or recovery space and clear expectations for its use. Give workers choices about desk location, collaboration, focus time and breaks where the work permits. Do not assume a quiet room is an appropriate adjustment for everyone.

## 4. Support, emergency arrangements and review

- [ ] **AW-17 (P/N/C)** Give people a simple, confidential route to request and review adjustments. Discuss barriers and preferred solutions with the person, agree who will act and when, and check that the adjustment works in real tasks. Include temporary and fluctuating needs, contractors and visitors as appropriate. Do not make a diagnosis or public disclosure a routine condition of discussing access. [Acas guidance](https://www.acas.org.uk/reasonable-adjustments/adjustments-for-neurodiversity) gives examples for neurodivergent workers.
- [ ] **AW-18 (P/N/C)** Make induction, safety instructions, policies, meeting invitations and feedback channels available in accessible formats. Explain unfamiliar spaces and equipment through orientation where wanted; train managers, reception and facilities staff to respond to requests without requiring public disclosure.
- [ ] **AW-19 (P/N/C)** Plan evacuation for everyone likely to use the premises, including people who cannot use stairs, hear an alarm or follow a visually indicated route. Agree individual arrangements where needed, provide appropriate audible, visual or vibrating alerts, maintain refuges and equipment where specified, and practise the plan. Never treat a refuge as the entire evacuation plan. Confirm arrangements with the responsible fire-safety lead and see the [government's means-of-escape guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fire-safety-risk-assessment-means-of-escape-for-disabled-people/fire-safety-risk-assessment-means-of-escape-for-disabled-people-accessible-version).
- [ ] **AW-20 (P/N/C)** Test a complete workday with disabled and neurodivergent workers, including arrival, desk work, meetings, breaks, toilets, technology failure and leaving. Involve people with different mobility, vision, hearing, communication and sensory experiences. Record barriers, owners and dates; confirm fixes with the people affected and compensate lived-experience contributors where appropriate.
- [ ] **AW-21 (P/N/C)** Recheck after refurbishment, a move, a new booking or security system, a change of role or a report of a barrier. Do not delay an employer's reasonable adjustment while exploring [Access to Work](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-guide-for-employers/access-to-work-factsheet-for-employers); that scheme may support needs beyond the employer's duty.

## Scope boundaries and connected guidance

This checklist concerns the **physical and operational workspace**. It does not cover the whole employment journey, including recruitment, onboarding beyond workspace orientation, progression, retention or exit. Use it alongside the [Disabled Employee Lifecycle repository](https://github.com/Double-FW/Disabled-Employee-Lifecycle) so workspace barriers are considered within the wider employee experience, not treated as isolated individual adjustments.

Use the [Organisation Governance and Strategy repository](https://github.com/Double-FW/Organisation_Governance_and_Strategy) to connect findings to organisational ownership, policy and governance. In particular, its [Accessibility RACI](https://github.com/Double-FW/Organisation_Governance_and_Strategy/blob/main/Accessibility%20RACI.md) can help clarify responsibilities. For conferences, staff gatherings and other corporate events, also use the [Inclusive Corporate Events repository](https://github.com/Double-FW/Inclusive-Corporate-Events), which covers attendee and presenter event journeys.
